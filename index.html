      Training mission and duel

      Sink the Bismarck

      Denmark Strait · 24 May 1941 · 0300

      A shakedown cruise ends in a time vortex. Tang surfaces in May 1941 with no GPS and only Morse on the radio. Bismarck and Prinz Eugen are breaking out into the Atlantic at 27 knots, with the British cruisers Suffolk and Norfolk shadowing them by radar. Bismarck's Arado floatplane scouts ahead. Sink Bismarck before she gets past you. Don't hit the British.

      Duel: Kazan

      Bear Gap · March 2029 · 0200

      Back in the present war. A Yasen-M, Kazan, is in your patrol box, quiet and armed, an Il-38N is laying sonobuoys overhead to cue it, and a Ka-27 helicopter is dipping its sonar. Find Kazan on passive sonar and sink it before they find you.

      • Stay slow to stay hidden. Above about 10 knots you get loud and your sonar goes deaf. The towed array hears farther and covers astern at 12 knots or less, once you've held a course for 90 seconds.
      • Identify by signature. Select a contact to see its machinery and propeller lines. The crew suggests classes with a match %. More lines appear as the contact gets louder or closer. Accept a match to classify it.
      • Look. At periscope depth you get an exact fix on ships within 8 miles, but they may spot your periscope. Click the ship (or press V) to see it through the periscope and compare it with the identification book.
      • Radar. At periscope depth, press R. Ships inside 15 miles are fixed and classified almost at once; farther out it is patchy. In 1941 nobody can hear it. In 2029 the Il-38's receiver can, and it will come looking. In 1941, radar is the only way to find aircraft.
      • Listen for aircraft. In 2029, sonobuoy splashes and a Ka-27's dipping sonar are on your sonar, and give away where the aircraft are. JATM and SM-6 are fire and forget: they fly down the selected air contact's bearing (or your heading) and take the first aircraft their seeker sees within 50 miles and 45° either side.
      • Build a solution. Range firms up the longer you track, and each new leg (a turn of 40° or more) improves it sharply.
      • Shoot. Mk 48s are wire-guided to your solution. Harpoon and Tomahawk fly to your solution and home on the ship nearest it, friend or foe, so a poor solution can hit the wrong ship. ASROC drops a Mk 54 on your solution 1 to 15 miles out. JATM and SM-6 shoot down aircraft. Missiles launch from 150 feet or shallower.
