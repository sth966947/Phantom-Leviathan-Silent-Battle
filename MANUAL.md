# Phantom Leviathan: Silent Battle · Captain's Manual

You command USS Tang (SSN-805), a Virginia Block V attack submarine. This manual covers the three campaigns, what Tang carries, and how to use every part of the game screen.

Open this manual any time with the **Manual** button on the campaign screen or the pause card, or press **?**.

## Quick start

1. Pick a campaign tab at the top of the campaign screen and press **Begin mission** (or **Take these orders**).
2. The game starts in real time. Press **Space** to pause at any point and read the briefing and the live objectives.
3. Stay slow (10 knots or less) and listen. Contacts appear in the **Contacts** list and on the plot.
4. Select a contact (click it, or press **Tab**), open the solution window (**B**) and identify it from its signature. Press **K** to accept the crew's top match.
5. Let the range firm up. Turning 40° or more starts a new leg and sharpens the range quickly.
6. Fire: **F** for a Mk 48 at the selected contact, or plan a timed salvo with the attack plan (**Shift+A**).
7. If a torpedo comes for you: countermeasure (**C**), turn away, speed up, and go deep under the layer.

Progress saves automatically in your browser. Each person who plays on their own computer and browser gets their own campaigns. Clearing browser data, or playing in a private window, loses it.

## The three campaigns

Pick a campaign with the three tabs at the top of the campaign screen. Each one keeps its own progress, so you can play all three side by side.

### WWII campaign · USS Tang, through the vortex

In 2029 Tang is pulled through a time vortex and comes out in May 1941, in the Denmark Strait. With no GPS, no satellites and Morse on the radio, she fights her way through the Second World War against enemies that can barely fight back. This is the training campaign: it teaches the sensors and weapons one mission at a time, with real historical ships, dates and places.

- **22 missions, in date order**, from Sink the Bismarck (May 1941) to Ten-Go: Yamato (April 1945). Winning a mission unlocks the next one.
- **History changes.** Ships you sink stay sunk. Sink a carrier at Pearl Harbor and she won't be at Midway. The campaign screen tells you what has changed before each mission, and after each mission a short interlude tells you how the war now stands.
- **Before the vortex (optional):** five stand-alone battles from 1939 to 1941 (River Plate, Narvik, Calabria, Taranto, Cape Matapan). They are open from the start and don't affect the story.
- **Present day:** Duel: Kazan, a one-on-one fight with a Russian Yasen-M submarine in the Bear Gap.
- **Restart campaign** clears every result and locks the campaign back to Sink the Bismarck. It takes two clicks.

The missions: Sink the Bismarck · The midget subs · Kido Butai · Force Z · Java Sea · Sunda Strait · The Doolittle Raid · Coral Sea · Midway · Convoy PQ-17 · Savo Island · Eastern Solomons · Santa Cruz Islands · Ironbottom Sound · Tassafaronga · Komandorski Islands · Convoy ONS-5 · North Cape · Philippine Sea · Leyte Gulf: Palawan Passage · Shinano · Ten-Go: Yamato.

WWII enemies have no modern sonar or missiles. Their aircraft can be found only by radar or the periscope, and attack only with torpedoes and depth charges. Escorts drop depth charges set for 50 to 400 feet.

### Modern war · 2029

Tang comes back from 1945 into a world at war: NATO against the BRICS Pact (Russia, China, Brazil, Iran, India and South Africa, bound by a mutual defence pact). The main enemies are Russia and China.

- **A branching war of 30 to 38 missions.** After each mission the result, and a radio bulletin about the rest of the war, decide where Tang is sent next. Sometimes you choose between two sets of orders.
- **Four fronts and escalation.** The bars at the top show the Atlantic, Pacific, Gulf and South Atlantic fronts, from −3 (BRICS winning) to +3 (NATO winning), and escalation from 0 to 5.
- **Acts:** Act 1 is the North Atlantic and the Arctic, Act 2 sends Tang to the Western Pacific or to the Indian Ocean and the Gulf, the Polar Front takes her under the ice with Japan against Russia and China, and Act 3 is the counter-offensive and the end.
- **Endings:** Victory, Stalemate, Defeat, or the Brink if escalation runs too high.
- **Nothing counts until you continue.** After a mission you can replay it, or press **Continue the war** to lock the result in. If Tang is lost you must replay the mission.
- **Restart war** starts a new war with new dice. A war keeps its dice when you reload, so reloading doesn't change what comes next.

Modern enemies are dangerous: submarines hunt you, patrol planes drop sonobuoys, helicopters dip their sonar, and escorts carry torpedoes and missiles. Some missions add minefields, pack ice, storms, land targets for Tomahawk strikes, or places you must reach (see Mission marks).

### Red Storm Rising · 1980s

The original story: the Warsaw Pact has gone to war with NATO, and the Soviet Northern Fleet is coming out of the Kola Inlet to cut Europe off from America. You fight for the Norwegian Sea.

- **Set up your war:** pick the **year** (1984, 1988, 1992 or 1996), the **boat**, and the **challenge** level, then press **Take command**.
- **Boats:** USS Tang with her full 2029 kit, or a 1980s attack boat: Permit (USS Flasher), Sturgeon (USS Pargo), Los Angeles (USS Dallas), Improved Los Angeles with vertical tubes (USS Newport News, 1988 on) or Seawolf (1996 only). Older boats carry the weapons of their year: the old Mk 48 and Harpoon in 1984; ADCAP, Tomahawks and land attack from 1988; Sea Lance in place of SUBROC and a mast-mounted Stinger from 1992. Older, louder boats count for more; Tang counts for least.
- **Challenge:** Introductory (deaf Soviet sonar, lighter damage), Normal, Serious (Soviet sonar 3 dB better, extra escorts) and Ultimate (sharp ears, heavy damage, and no crew matches: you identify every contact yourself). Harder levels score more.
- **The gauge** runs from "Warsaw Pact holds the sea" to "NATO holds the sea". Your results and the news from the front move it, and where it stands decides which Soviet forces come out next. The war ends when one side holds the whole sea, or in a ceasefire after 24 missions.
- **Efficiency rating (ER):** each mission scores the targets you sank, other ships, aircraft and land hits, weighted by your boat and challenge level. A good mission earns a medal, from the Navy Commendation Medal to the Medal of Honor, and your career ER raises your rank from Commander to Admiral.
- **Battle sims:** single fights that don't count toward the war, from the button on the setup screen.

This campaign uses the original Soviet names, since it fights the Soviets.

## USS Tang (SSN-805)

A Virginia-class Block V attack submarine, and the quietest boat in any of the campaigns. Values in the game are invented for play, not real data.

| | |
|---|---|
| Top speed | 32 knots (Flank) |
| Depth | Periscope depth (60 ft) down to 800 ft in the game |
| Torpedo tubes | 4, reload 2 minutes after each shot |
| Vertical launch tubes | 12, reload 10 minutes after each shot; any tube takes any vertical weapon |
| Countermeasures | 4 ADC Mk 5 |
| Drones | 4 Blackwing (air), 6 Iver4 UUVs (underwater) |
| Sonar | Hull sonar, plus a TB-34 towed array |

Weapon counts are unlimited in this version of the game. Only the tubes limit how fast you fire.

**Noise.** At 10 knots or less Tang is close to silent. Above that she gets loud quickly, and her own sonar goes deaf at the same time. Torpedoes (Mk 48 and Mk 96) swim quietly out of the tube; missile launches are heard by anyone listening. Enemy torpedo launches are loud.

## Weapons

The Weapons card has two tabs: **Standard** and **Experimental**. The ` key switches tabs, and firing a weapon from the keyboard brings up its tab. Weapons fire at the selected contact using your current solution, so a poor solution means a miss, or a hit on the wrong ship.

Missiles launch from **150 ft or shallower**. Torpedoes fire at any depth.

### Standard

| Weapon | Tube | Key | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mk 48 ADCAP Mod 7 | Torpedo | F | Ships and subs | 55 kt, 22 nm. Wire-guided to your solution until its seeker (2.5 nm) acquires. The wire parts if Tang goes faster than 24 kt or the torpedo is more than 16 nm away; it then runs on its own. |
| NSM (Naval Strike Missile) | Torpedo | H | Surface ships | Out to 100 nm. Stealthy and sea-skimming, so point defence seldom stops it. Its infrared seeker picks a weak point: the screws, the bridge, or the magazine. |
| Tomahawk anti-ship (Maritime Strike) | Vertical | T | Surface ships | Long range, updated in flight. Flies to your solution and homes on the ship nearest it, friend or foe. |
| ASROC | Vertical | G | Submarines (ships at a pinch) | Flies 1 to 15 nm to your solution and drops a Mk 54 torpedo there. |
| AIM-260 JATM | Vertical | J | Slow sub-hunting aircraft (helicopters, patrol planes) | Seven rounds to a canister, one canister per tube. Fire and forget. |
| SM-6 | Vertical | M | Incoming missiles and fast jets, and any aircraft | Longer reach. Fire and forget. |
| Tomahawk land attack (TLAM) | Vertical | Y | Fixed sites ashore | Shown only on missions with land targets. Pick the site from the list, then fire. Flies by terrain matching, so it needs no GPS. |
| Mines | Torpedo | I | Enemy shipping | Shown only on missions that carry mines. Lays a mine where Tang is, at 12 knots or less. |

**JATM and SM-6** fly down the selected air contact's bearing (or straight ahead if nothing is selected), and take the first aircraft their seeker sees within 50 nm and 45° either side. If nothing is there they fly on until their fuel runs out.

### Experimental

Tang is trialling three weapons nobody else has.

| Weapon | Tube | Key | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mk 96 supercavitating torpedo | Torpedo | Shift+F | Swims out quietly at 50 kt on the wire. Once its seeker acquires, it lights its rocket and runs in at 250 kt: it can't be outrun or decoyed, but it is very loud and has only 15 nm of fuel. |
| Mjolnir (sub-launched PrSM Increment 2), ship | Vertical | Shift+T | A ballistic arc to the target 10 to 1,000 nm away, then down at Mach 6 with a 1,500 lb penetrating warhead. Air defence can reach it only as it climbs or dives, and seldom stops it. Hits like three Tomahawks. |
| Mjolnir, land | Vertical | Shift+Y | The same missile at a fixed site ashore. Three Tomahawks' worth of damage, and it rarely misses. Shown only on missions with land targets. |

### Attack plan (Shift+A)

The attack plan fires coordinated salvoes. It pauses the game while it is open.

- It lists every hostile ship, submarine, aircraft and land target with bearing and range.
- For each target, step the **weapon** and the **number** (1 to 4) with the arrows, the arrow keys or the mouse wheel.
- Press **Fire salvoes**. Everything fires at once; nothing waits in a tube.
- Each weapon type is its own salvo, timed to hit on the same second. NSM and Tomahawks loiter near the boat as long as their fuel allows, Mjolnir and ASROC fly a longer arc, and torpedoes run slow. Different weapon types are not timed against each other.
- If a target runs and some torpedoes fall behind, the rest of that salvo slows to match.

### Battle camera

Every hit on a ship, submarine, aircraft or target ashore plays a short clip. Hits that land close together play as one reel. The game waits while the clip plays. Press **Esc** to skip it.

## Drones

The Drones card has two tabs: **Blackwing** and **UUV**.

### Blackwing (air)

A small reconnaissance drone fired from the 3-inch signal ejector, at any depth. Four aboard.

- **Launch (Shift+B):** the canister floats up (about 30 seconds) and the drone flies at 55 kt to the selected contact (or down its bearing, or ahead) and circles it.
- **What it does:** its camera fixes, tracks and identifies every ship and aircraft it can see, 10 nm by day and 7 nm by night, for an hour. Aircraft it tracks can be engaged with JATM or SM-6. Enemies can't find it.
- **Data link:** the pictures reach Tang within 50 nm at periscope depth, or 30 nm at 150 ft or shallower. Deeper than that, the link is down.
- **Steer (Shift+D):** while you steer a drone, clicks on the plot go to it: click water to send it there, or a ship to follow it. Press again for the next drone. **Esc** goes back to steering the boat. You can also click a drone on the plot.
- **To target:** sends the drone you are steering (or the newest) to the selected contact.
- **Radar search:** listens for enemy search radars out to 60 nm and works out what and where each one is. It has no radar of its own, so nothing can see it doing this.
- **Jam radar (Shift+J):** jams enemy radar within 15 nm of the drone. Ships and aircraft there can't see your periscope or missile launches on radar, and their radar-guided air defence has half the chance against your missiles. Radar search is off while it jams.

### Iver4 UUV (underwater)

A quiet battery-powered underwater drone, swum out of a torpedo tube. Six aboard.

- **Launch (O):** heads toward the selected contact (or ahead), runs 4 nm and hovers until you steer it. A launch never needs an empty tube: it counts as a torpedo-tube shot, and if every tube is reloading it goes out of the first one to clear.
- **Acoustic link:** talks to Tang out to 15 nm.
- **Steer (Shift+U):** clicks on the plot go to the UUV: click water to send it there, or a ship or submarine to shadow it. **Esc** returns to the boat.
- **Shadow (Shift+W):** the UUV sprints at 14 kt to the selected contact, then trails 0.8 nm astern matching her course, speed and depth, and passes back an exact fix for targeting. Select another contact and press again to switch; steer it to open water to pull it off.
- **Recover (Shift+R):** calls the UUV home. Slow to 8 kt or less and it comes back aboard through a torpedo tube, recharged. That tube then reloads as if it had fired.
- **Modes** (Shift+O cycles them):
  - **Passive:** silent extra ears away from the boat, and a cross-fix when both it and Tang hear a contact.
  - **Active:** pings every minute. Hunters go for the drone, not for Tang.
  - **Decoy:** plays Tang's noise. With a torpedo after it, it runs away from Tang at 14 kt, towing the seeker along.
- **Mine hunting:** its high-frequency sonar finds mines within 1 nm.

Active and decoy modes run the battery down three times as fast as passive.

## Sensors

### Passive sonar

Tang hears contacts by their noise. Passive sonar gives a **bearing only**: the range starts as a rough guess and firms up the longer you track, and sharply each time you start a new leg (turn 40° or more). A contact is easier to hear when it is loud, close or fast, and harder when you are fast.

- **Baffles:** the hull sonar can't hear in a 60° arc straight astern.
- **Towed array (TB-34):** hears farther and covers astern, but only at 12 kt or less and once you have held a course for 90 seconds.
- **Thermal layer:** sound loses a lot crossing the layer, for you and the enemy alike. Hide below it; listen above it.
- **Straddling the layer:** **Sub below · array above (U)** takes Tang to 550 ft under the layer with the towed array up above it, to hear surface ships while staying hidden. **Sub above · array below (N)** keeps Tang at 300 ft above the layer with the array deep below it, to hear submarines hiding under the layer.

### Contact ID by signature

Every class of ship and submarine has its own machinery lines and propeller blade count. Select a contact and open the solution window (**B**) to see its narrowband signature. Lines appear one by one as the contact gets louder or closer. The crew suggests the classes that fit, with a match percentage. Accept a match (**K** accepts the top one) to label the contact; friendly classes turn green.

### Active ping (P)

Gives an exact fix on everything the ping bounces off, and lights up enemy mines nearby. But anyone with sonar within 60 nm hears where it came from. Use it sparingly.

### Periscope

At periscope depth Tang gets an exact fix and a sight of ships within 8 nm and aircraft within 10 nm (less in rough seas). Click a ship you can see, or press **V**, to look at it through the periscope and compare it with the identification book. Enemy ships within 6 nm may spot the periscope; the group then turns away and the escorts come hunting.

### Radar (R)

Mast radar, at periscope depth only. Switch it on and off by hand: emitting gives Tang away to enemies with radar warning receivers.

- Ships are seen and identified out to 50 nm.
- Low fliers such as helicopters show inside 15 nm; high fliers out to 100 nm. Aircraft are identified inside 15 nm.
- In 1941 nobody can detect it, and it is the only way to find aircraft.

### Hearing aircraft

In the modern campaigns, sonobuoy splashes and a helicopter's dipping sonar are on your sonar, and give away where the aircraft are. Heard sonobuoys show as small red squares on the plot.

## Defence

- **Countermeasure (C):** launches an ADC Mk 5 noisemaker. Four aboard. Drop one, then turn away and speed up so the torpedo chases the noise.
- **Evading torpedoes:** an incoming torpedo shows on the plot as a red bearing line with sonar's estimate of where it is, where it is heading, and its range, speed and time to you. Turn away, go under the layer, and use a countermeasure or a UUV decoy.
- **Depth charges:** WWII escorts set them for 50 to 400 ft. Go to 700 ft and slow down.
- **Mines:** enemy mines show on the plot once sonar has found them. Slow down to let the high-frequency sonar find them farther out, ping, or send a UUV ahead.

## The campaign screen

- **Campaign tabs** along the top switch between WWII, Modern war and Red Storm Rising.
- **The list** on the left shows missions: ✓ won, ✗ lost, greyed out if locked.
- **The detail** on the right shows the briefing for the selected mission and the button to begin.
- **Manual** opens this manual.

## The game screen

### Header

- **Pause / Resume (Space):** pausing brings up the **pause card**.
- **Back to campaign:** leaves the mission. Mid-mission it takes two clicks so a stray one doesn't throw it away.
- **Clock:** the time of day where Tang is.
- **Mission time:** how long since the mission began.

### Pause card

Shows the mission briefing and a live checklist of the objectives (○ open, ✓ done, ✗ failed).

- **Resume (Space)** carries on.
- **Hide, stay paused** clears the card so you can study the plot while paused.
- **Manual** opens this manual.
- When every objective is met, the card says **Mission accomplished**. **End mission** finishes now; **Keep fighting** lets you carry on. Everything you sink still counts.

### Own ship card (left)

- **Heading, speed, depth** (and whether you are above or below the layer), **radiated noise**, and **towed array** state.
- **Match:** with a contact selected, **Course** steers its course, **Bearing** steers straight at it, and **Speed (Z)** keeps pace with it. They keep following until you steer or order a speed yourself, or select another contact. Course and Bearing can't both be on.
- **Speed buttons:** Creep 3, ⅓ 5, ⅔ 10, Std 15, Full 20, Flank 32 knots.
- **Depth buttons:** Periscope (60 ft), 150 ft, 700 ft. **▲ 50** and **▼ 50** change depth by 50 ft, or type a depth from 60 to 800 ft and press **Go**.

### Control room log

Everything the crew reports: new contacts, launches, hits, warnings. Contacts are amber, danger red, good news green.

### Tactical plot (centre)

A north-up chart centred on Tang, with range rings and a compass rose.

- **Click open water** to steer toward that bearing. **Click a contact** to select it (and bring up the periscope view if you can see it).
- **Scroll** or **+ / −** to zoom.
- **Tang** is blue, with her track behind her and the ordered heading ahead. The grey wedge astern is the baffles, or a blue line when the towed array is working.
- **Contacts** are amber (green once identified as friendly), with a bearing line, the estimated position and a range uncertainty. Once the solution is good, a leader shows the contact's course and where it will be in six minutes (one minute for aircraft).
- **Your weapons** are tracked on the plot. **Incoming torpedoes** are red.
- **Rings:** periscope range at periscope depth, and the radar ID range with a sweeping trace when radar is on.
- **Drones:** Blackwings with the circle their camera covers and their link state, UUVs as small squares with their mode. The drone you are steering has a ring.
- **Mission marks:** places in the briefing (mine zones, exits, rendezvous points, keep-out rings) are green; land targets are red squares. Marks off the edge of the plot show as pointers on the rim with their name and range.
- **Pack ice** shows as a pale wash, with open water (polynyas) ringed. Under ice Tang can't come shallow except in open water.
- After a mission the plot shows where every ship really was.

**Depth view (X):** a side-on cut through the sea around Tang: the sky, the surface, the thermal layer, and each contact at its estimated range and depth or altitude. Contacts with no range yet stack in a column on the right.

### Time compression (below the plot)

**Pause, ×1, ×10, ×30, ×90** (keys Space, 1, 2, 3, 4). Time compression never runs past a hit: the game stops there to play the battle camera.

### Contacts card (right)

- Every contact with its designation or class, bearing and range. Struck-through contacts are sunk; faded ones are lost.
- Click a row or press **Tab** to select.
- Below the list: the selected contact's propeller reading and the crew's **possible matches** with a match bar and an **Accept** button (**K** for the top one).
- **Build solution (B)** opens the solution window.

### Solution window (B)

- **Broadband waterfall:** everything sonar hears, by true bearing, newest at the top.
- **Signature:** the selected contact's narrowband lines (frequency across, time down).
- **What the sensors have:** bearing and drift, range and its error, which sensor holds it, the lines and blade count seen, and whether it is cavitating.
- **Possible matches table:** each class still possible, with how many lines match, blades, top speed and the crew's match %.
- **Recognition book:** pick a class to see its picture, photo, reference signature (your contact's lines show as ticks along the top for comparison), facts and notes, and **Accept** it.

### Periscope view (V)

The selected ship or aircraft as it looks at its real range and angle, with the identification book alongside to compare shapes and accept a class. **V** or **Esc** lowers it.

### Weapons card

Standard and Experimental tabs (see Weapons), the tube status (what is ready and what is reloading), and the **Attack plan** button.

### Sensors and defence card

Radar state, countermeasures left, and buttons for **Periscope view (V)**, **Radar (R)**, **Active ping (P)**, **Countermeasure (C)**, and the two layer-straddling orders (**U**, **N**).

### Drones card

Blackwing and UUV tabs (see Drones), with the number aboard, link state and mode.

## Tips

- **Slow is quiet.** Creep or ⅓ while you search. Sprint only when you must, and slow down again to listen.
- **Turn to get range.** Two legs at least 40° apart give a much better solution than one long leg.
- **Identify before you shoot.** Missiles home on the ship nearest your solution, friend or foe.
- **Use the layer.** Below it you are hard to hear from the surface; above it you hear the surface better.
- **Let the drones look.** A Blackwing over the enemy fleet gives exact fixes without Tang showing a mast; a shadowing UUV gives a perfect fire-control solution on a submarine.
- **Pause often.** Space pauses the game and shows the objectives; nothing happens while you think.
